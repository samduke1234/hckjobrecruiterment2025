About High Court of Karnataka

After the death of Tipu Sultan in 1799, the British restored the throne to Krishnaraja Wodeyar III, marking the revival of the Wodeyar dynasty. Under his reign, Mysore became a beacon of culture, education, and economic growth, with initiatives to modernize administration, promote learning, and develop infrastructure.

In 1831, due to allegations of administrative mismanagement, the British assumed direct control over Mysore. A British Commissioner oversaw governance, introducing reforms in revenue collection, judicial systems, and public works. Though under colonial oversight, Mysore witnessed significant development in irrigation, roads, and institutional frameworks.

Restoration and Modern Administration

In 1881, Chamarajendra Wodeyar X was reinstated as ruler. A Diwan-led administrative system was introduced, assisted by a council of ministers, blending traditional monarchy with modern bureaucracy. Mysore thrived as a model princely state, pioneering education, healthcare, and industrial reforms.

The rulers and Diwans promoted:

  • Irrigation projects and agriculture development
  • Sericulture and cottage industries
  • Arts, literature, and cultural preservation
  • Educational institutions and healthcare initiatives

Integration into Independent India

In 1947, Mysore joined the Dominion of India through the Instrument of Accession. It became a Part 'B' State, with the Maharaja serving as the ceremonial Rajpramukh. Democratic institutions began shaping governance while preserving Mysore’s rich cultural and administrative heritage.

Legacy and Modern Relevance

Today, Mysore stands as a symbol of cultural richness and progressive governance. Its historical commitment to welfare, education, and industry continues to inspire modern initiatives in:

  • Urban development and technology
  • Heritage conservation and tourism
  • Education, healthcare, and socio-economic development
  • Cultural festivals and arts promotion

Mysore’s journey exemplifies the harmonious blending of tradition and modernity, reflecting the vision of the Wodeyar dynasty and their commitment to public welfare and state development.
